 Group 10 
 Event Ticketing and Check-in System
Source: Interview with Organiser of Linkedin Local, Cameroon.

How registration worked For Them
Events are advertised on WhatsApp groups and Facebook. People register through a Google Form (name, phone number, email), or by WhatsApp if the form is hard for them. The organisers then copy everything into an Excel sheet. Participants get a confirmation message by WhatsApp or email. There is no unique ticket.

Payment
Participants pay by MTN Mobile Money or Orange Money to the organiser's number and forward a screenshot. Organisers check each payment by hand and mark it in the Excel sheet. Screenshots often arrive without a name, so organisers have to contact the sender to find out who paid.
At the entrance
Three people work the door: one welcomes, one checks registration and payment, one solves problems. They search a printed list by name and tick it.

Problems reported
•	The first 30 minutes are the busiest. Searching the list by hand creates queues.
•	Names are spelled differently at the door than on the registration form.
•	One person said they had registered and paid but was not on the list. Entry was delayed until an organiser confirmed the payment and registration.
•	Attendance counts are not fully reliable, because names are sometimes not ticked during the rush.
•	Contacts are kept in Excel and on paper, with no central system. They are reused for thank-you messages and invitations.

Numbers
About 500 people registered and about 425 attended. Three people staffed the entrance.

What the organiser wants
Every registered person gets a unique ticket with a QR code. At the door, staff scan it to confirm registration and payment, and the system marks
 the person as present. The organiser expects this to save time and cut mistakes.

What this means for our system
•	Generate a unique ticket (QR code) when someone registers.
•	Scan the ticket at the entrance and mark attendance automatically.
•	Show payment status at check-in.
•	Allow a name search as a fallback that copes with different spellings.
•	Give organisers an attendance count and a list of contacts afterwards.
