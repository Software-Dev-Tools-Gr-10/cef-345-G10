# **Field Notes: Visit to Mr Ngum's Shop** 

Chung Silas Nditeck  |  Group 10, Event Ticketing and Check-in System  |  Week 4 

**Date and time:** 14/11/2026 **Place:** Mile-17 New-lay-out Buea 

## **What the business is** 

Mr Ngum runs a small shop selling food items such as tomatoes and rice. He works mostly on his own and serves customers directly as they come in. 

## **What I observed** 

- He sells the goods himself and writes each sale by hand in an exercise book. 

- The exercise book is the only record of what he sells. There is no other system behind it. 

- Recording a sale means stopping to write, which slows him down when customers are waiting. 

## **What came out of my questions** 

I asked open questions about how he handles sales and what goes wrong. The main points were: 

- He keeps track of sales and stock manually, and finding or adding up information in the book takes effort. 

- Some customers take goods on credit, and he often forgets who borrowed and how much, because the debts are not recorded properly. 

- He has no quick way to know how much he has sold in a day or when an item is running low. Problems I identified 

- Unrecorded or forgotten credit leads to lost money. 

- A handwritten book is slow to use and easy to misread, lose or damage. 

- Nothing warns him when stock is low or helps him see his profit. 

## **What this means for our system** 

A better system would let him record a sale quickly, keep a clear list of who owes him and how much, and show him his stock and daily sales without adding up by hand. It would need to be very simple to use, since he is busy serving customers all day.
